ISORIM - developer notes (version 1.0, built for RimWorld 1.5)
==============================================

How to build the mod, how it works, and how to change or add models. For players, see
README.txt. To make the folder that goes on the Steam Workshop: run build.bat, then
make_release.bat; upload ../IsoRim_Release from the game's mod uploader.


1. BUILD IT (once, and again after any code change)
----------------------------------------------------
  a. Install the free ".NET SDK" from https://dotnet.microsoft.com/download
  b. Double-click build.bat (Windows) or run build.sh (macOS/Linux).
  c. Success leaves IsoRim.dll in the Assemblies folder. Without that file the mod does
     nothing at all, however it is enabled.
     If it says BUILD FAILED, the window lists the errors.


2. INSTALL IT
-------------
  a. Subscribe to the "Harmony" mod on the Steam Workshop.
  b. Put this whole IsoRim folder in RimWorld's Mods folder.
  c. In the game's Mods screen enable Harmony and IsoRim, with Harmony above IsoRim.


3. USE IT
---------
  0         switch the 3D view on and off (the number row, not the keypad)
  9         swap between the isometric view and the straight-on view (north up)
  Q and E   turn the camera left / right, smoothly: 45 degrees a step, or 90 if
            "Turn in 45° steps" is unticked in the options
            (while something is picked to build, Q and E turn that instead, as usual)
  [ and ]   lower / raise the camera angle
  (no key)  show roofs on and off; set a key for it if you like
  Bottom right corner of the screen, next to the game's own buttons: a cube button
  for the 3D view and, while it is on, a house button for the roofs.
  Hold the pointer near a wall to lower the walls round it and see behind them.
  All the keys can be changed in Options > Keyboard configuration > IsoRim.
  Options > Mod options > IsoRim   four tabs: View and camera, Colour and light, Ground,
                                  water and roofs, and What is 3D. The header there has a
                                  button that turns the 3D view on, in case the key does
                                  nothing, and one that puts every option back to default.

  It is visual only and does not touch saves.


4. WHAT IT DOES
---------------
  - Tilts the map camera and fixes mouse clicks and off-screen culling to match.

  - Isometric view (the default; key 9 swaps to the straight-on view and back): the camera
    is turned 45 degrees and lowered to the true isometric angle, so the map's grid runs
    diagonally and two sides of every wall and building show. Moving the camera with the
    keys, the screen edges or by dragging follows the screen, not the map; upright sprites
    turn to face the camera; dragging a selection box picks what is inside the box; name
    labels sit under their colonists.

  - Q and E swing the camera round the middle of the screen, an eighth of a turn at a time
    (or a quarter, as an option), easing in and out, so a base can be seen from every
    side. Turning the camera never changes the light: the sun stays put in the world.
    It rises in the east, stands in the south at noon and sets in the west, moving on to
    a new place in the sky a few times a game day (and a fixed moonlight at night). When
    it moves, walls, furniture and roofs are relit a few blocks a frame, the part on
    screen first. "Sun moves through the day" in the options keeps it still instead.

  - Walls and barricades are drawn as 3D blocks with the mod's own textures (bricks,
    planks, metal, smooth stone), tinted by what they are built from, with dark outlines.

  - Walls lower near the pointer: every built wall and door within a few cells of the
    pointer (the "Reach round the pointer" slider) drops to half height, so what is behind
    can be seen and clicked. Move away and they stand up again. The height left is a
    slider too. Natural rock is never lowered. The old behaviour, where every
    wall in front of a roofed room is lowered all the time, is still there as a tick box
    ("Cut down walls in front of every room"), off by default.

  - Fences are 3D: a post in every cell with two rails reaching to each neighbouring fence,
    gate or wall. A fence gate is two posts with a braced leaf that swings open.

  - Sandbags are four courses of heavy, squared-off bags stacked like bricks, flattened by
    the weight above, pinched at the tied ends, stepping in as they rise, in the colour of
    what they are made from. A row turns to follow its own line.

  - Wall torches and wall lamps are 3D and hang on the face of their wall, lower on a wall
    that is cut down.

  - Natural rock is drawn the same way but with broken edges: each exposed face leans
    back, kinks part of the way up and meets a lowered, uneven rim, so a mountain reads as
    a cliff and not as a stack of cubes. Ore-bearing rock has its own texture.

  - Doors are 3D: two leaves as tall as the walls that slide apart into the walls on either
    side, under a beam that carries the wall line across the doorway. Both leaves are lit
    the same.

  - Loose rock chunks are 3D boulders. There are six shapes, each chunk keeps its own shape,
    turn and size, and they take the colour of the stone. Steel slag has its own two shapes.

  - Weapons are 3D models, both lying on the ground and in a colonist's hands: 21 firearm,
    bow and grenade models and 9 melee models covering the base game, with a stand-in rifle
    or sword for any other weapon from DLC or mods. Blades take the colour of their metal.

  - Clothing lying on the ground is a 3D model by kind: folded garment, hat, helmet, body
    armour, belt or pack. Clothing a colonist is wearing is part of the colonist's sprite
    and is not modelled (see the known gaps).

  - People are 3D figures in the game's own proportions: a big round head on a simple body.
    Everything the game makes up for a person is read from them, so each figure matches its
    colonist, raider or visitor:
      build      the five body types, with children and babies smaller
      head       narrow, average or wide, in the person's skin colour
      hair       the person's hair colour, in the nearest of eight cuts (short, medium,
                 long, afro, mohawk, ponytail, bun, spiky) or bald
      beard      full or goatee, in the hair colour
      clothes    shirt and trousers colour the upper and lower body; a jacket, or a long
                 coat for dusters, parkas and robes, goes over them; armour adds shoulder
                 plates; all in the colour of the actual garment
      headgear   cap, brimmed hat, tall hat, hood, open helmet, or a sealed helmet with a
                 visor, in the colour of the actual item
    A figure turns to face the way it is really walking and waddles in step, lies on its
    back when asleep or downed, and shows only its head above the covers in a bed. A weapon
    is carried in 3D at its side. Untick "Colonists and other people" in the options to get the
    upright sprites back.

  - Trees are 3D models that grow with the tree and lose their leaves in winter. Each kind
    is built after the game's own tree: broad oaks, red maples, tall teak, white-barked
    birch with dark bands, columns of poplar and cypress, tiered pines, willows with hanging
    curtains, palms with fronds and coconuts, cecropia, cocoa with pods, the flat-topped
    drago, anima, gauranlen, ribbed saguaro and jointed bamboo. Every tree has a real
    skeleton of trunk, boughs and twigs, so it is still a tree when bare, and a crown in
    three tones of its leaf colour. Each tree keeps its own turn and size. Trees have a
    much finer dark outline than buildings, and it no longer thickens as the tree grows.

  - Soft shading on the ground under everything 3D: walls, rock, furniture, boulders,
    items, trees and animals. It is darkest at the foot of the thing and fades out a short
    way, day and night.

  - Trees cast shadows: the outline of the crown plus a bar for the trunk, drawn with the
    game's own shadow system, so they swing round and stretch through the day, fade at night
    and match the shadows of walls and colonists. A bare winter tree casts only its trunk.
    "Tree shadow length" in the options sets how far they reach.

  - Animals are 3D models in the same faceted, outlined style: 60 of the base game's
    animals, from chickens and squirrels to muffalo, elephants, thrumbos and the three
    insects. They are drawn the way the game draws them, not the way the real animal
    looks: a big head, big eyes, a round body and short stout legs. A model turns to face the way the animal is really walking (a sprite only
    has four facings), its legs swing in step with the ground it covers, young animals are
    smaller, a sleeping animal settles onto its belly and a downed or dead one lies on its
    side. Animals from DLC and mods, and mechanoids, keep their upright sprites.

  - Grass is a soft, fluffy tuft: three rounded puffs of leaf and a broad arching blade or
    two, a little less saturated than before and drawn without an outline. Kept to about
    fifty triangles each, as a meadow holds thousands.

  - Wild plants and crops are small 3D models in the same faceted, outlined style:
    grass, tall grass, bushes, berry bushes, brambles, moss, dandelions, astragalus,
    daylilies, roses, agave, cactus, healroot, potatoes, strawberries, rice, haygrass, corn,
    cotton, hops, smokeleaf, psychoid, devilstrand and cave mushrooms. Any other plant that
    is not a tree (DLC, mods) gets a stand-in clump coloured from its own sprite.
    Each plant is scattered off the middle of its cell and keeps its own turn and size.

  - Natural ground is repainted to match: packed dirt, sand, gravel, mud, ice, and rough,
    rough-hewn and smooth stone, each with broad facets and finer detail inside them (small
    facets, pebbles, grit, hairline cracks). Only the picture is swapped, and only while
    the 3D view is on.

  - Soil is drawn as grass: soft, muted green tones with tufts painted as fluffy, leafy
    clumps with a few blade tips round the edge, the same from every side the camera can
    turn to. A noise map lets bare
    earth show through in ragged patches of every size (about a sixth of the area). Rich
    soil is a darker, lusher grass with less earth, so good farmland still stands out. It is
    only a picture: the ground is still soil to the game. Untick "Soil is drawn as grass" in
    the options to get plain brown earth back.

  - Marshy soil is wet ground in the same style: dark mud and dull green grass in patches,
    flat-coloured pools of standing water with a darker rim, and sparse tufts of reed.
    Moss is a fine, even green of small rosettes.

  - Colour looks over the whole 3D view: Gentle (the mod's usual, slightly muted look),
    Warm afternoon, Cool morning, Filmic, Bleak rim, Vivid, Autumn, Moonlight, Toxic,
    Sepia and Noir, with a strength slider, or Custom with colour and brightness sliders.
    The options show each look on a row of sample colours. They work out of the box by
    blending a few see-through sheets over the picture. For exact grading with lookup
    tables (LUTs), see "Exact colour grading" below. The interface is not affected.

  - Roofs: the house button in the bottom right corner (or a key) shows every roof at
    once as a solid lid at the top of the walls. A built roof rises from every edge to a ridge, textured in courses of slates,
    terracotta tiles, thatch or corrugated tin (picked in the options), lit by the sun,
    runs out over the walls that hold it with a short overhang and a fascia board, and
    dims with the daylight; a natural rock roof over a cave is a stone lid. Press the
    button again to see inside. Building or removing a roof updates it straight away.

  - Every buildable thing in the base game now has a hand-made model (new in this version
    unless listed below): hospital beds, bedrolls, animal beds; billiards, chess and poker
    tables, hoopstone ring, horseshoes pin; tube, flatscreen and megascreen televisions;
    telescope, tool cabinet, vitals monitor; small, large and grand sculptures (three
    designs each, in the sculpture's own material); coloured standing lamps; the nutrient
    paste dispenser and hoppers; biofuel refinery, fabrication bench, crematorium, hi-tech
    research bench, multi-analyzer; deep drill, ground-penetrating and long-range mineral
    scanners; comms console, orbital trade beacon, moisture pump; cryptosleep caskets,
    transport pods and pod launcher; the geothermal, watermill and chemfuel generators,
    power switch, cooler, vent and passive cooler; mini-turret, autocannon, sniper turret
    and mortar, each with a 3D gun that turns to aim; spike traps, every kind of IED and
    the firefoam popper; and the ship's beams, computer core, reactor, engines and sensor.
    Each is built for the footprint it has in the game and stretched to fit if a thing's
    real size differs, so mod-changed sizes still fit.

  - Items lying on the ground are models too: stacked steel, plasteel, uranium and gold
    bars, silver coins, jade, log piles, component crates, fuel cans, the three medicines,
    every meal, pemmican, kibble, potatoes, rice, corn, berries, fungus, agave, hops, meat,
    eggs, milk, insect jelly, chocolate, beer, hay, cloth, leather, wool, stone blocks, the
    drugs, mortar shells, neurotrainers, techprints, body parts and artifacts.

  - Ship chunks, mechanoid slag, and the wrecks and junk other content scatters over maps
    (rusted cars and car frames, jeeps, trucks, tanks, APCs, war-walker remains, barrels,
    crates, pipelines, engine blocks, dead machinery, lampposts, cannon barrels and
    tripods, concrete barriers, razor wire, scrap heaps) have models. They are matched by
    name, so the wrecks from DLC and mods are picked up whatever they are called exactly.

  - Furniture and machines with hand-made, textured 3D models: beds (pillow under the
    sleeper's head), tables, chairs, dresser, lamps, plant pot, column, battery, heater,
    wood-fired generator, a solar array of six framed panels on a braced stand, and a wind
    turbine with a mast and three blades that turn faster the more power it makes.
    New in this version: the work benches (butcher table, hand and electric tailoring,
    fueled and electric smithy, machining table, fueled and electric stove, stonecutter,
    art bench, drug lab, brewery, electric smelter and the simple research bench),
    each with its own tools on top, shelves in both sizes, the hydroponics basin, the sun
    lamp, the sarcophagus and the fermenting barrel.

  - The campfire is a ring of stones round a stack of logs with a real fire on it: three
    tongues of flame and a bright heart that flicker, each to its own beat, and puffs of
    grey smoke that rise, swell, lean with the wind and thin away. A fire that has run out
    of fuel has neither.

  - A steam geyser is a cracked mound of pale rock round a dark vent, with a tall column of
    white steam puffs. A geothermal generator built on it caps the steam.

  - Graves are 3D: an open pit edged with earth until someone is buried, then a mound with
    a headstone.

  - Built floors are repainted in the same style: wood boards, stone tiles, flagstones,
    carpet, concrete, paving, metal plates (steel, silver, gold) and sterile tile.

  - Water is drawn in the same hand as the pools in the marsh ground: flat dark teal, a
    little lighter near the bank and darker out in the deep, a thin pale line just inside
    the bank, the ground darkening toward the water, inside corners of the bank curved,
    faint streaks along the current of rivers, and little stars of light winking on it.

  - Marshy ground now uses the mod's own marsh picture (pools, mud and reeds). It was
    being treated as water before, so the picture was never used.

  - Everything else becomes 3D automatically: its own game sprite is raised to become the
    top, with sides dropped from the sprite's outline. Items on a shelf sit on top of it.

  - Animals and people that have no model (see the known gaps) stand upright as sprites and
    face the camera. A potted plant grows from the rim of its pot.

  - Flames, charge bars, turret heads and status icons (no power, forbidden ...) are moved
    up to sit on top of the raised building they belong to.

  - Night and indoor darkness still applies to raised things. Unexplored areas are capped
    at wall height.


5. KNOWN GAPS
-------------
  - The 3D people are simple: no arms or legs, as in the game's own art, and no attack,
    work or carrying animations. A figure replaces everything the game draws on the
    person's sprite, so wounds, tattoos, body modifications such as horns and tails, and
    whatever they are hauling in their arms do not show. Clothes show as colours and a few
    shapes, not as each garment's own cut, and belts and packs are left out.
  - Hair, beards and hats are matched to the nearest shape by their names. A style with an
    unusual name falls back to short hair, a full beard or a cap; say which ones look wrong.
  - Only baseline humans (and their xenotypes) get figures. Alien races from mods and
    mechanoids keep their upright sprites, and invisible people stay invisible.
  - People, animals, flames and smoke are relit in eight steps as they turn, and each
    differently coloured figure is its own small mesh. A very large crowd of people in
    many different clothes costs some memory; untick "Colonists and other people" if it drags.
  - Q and E also turn a building that is being placed. The mod leaves them alone while
    something is picked to build, so put the building tool away to turn the camera. If
    another mod already uses Q and E, rebind the two camera keys.
  - The lowered walls follow the pointer in steps of two cells, and the patch of map they
    are in is redrawn each time, so on a slow machine sweeping the pointer along a very
    long wall may stutter slightly. Untick "Lower walls near the pointer" if so.
  - Smoke and steam are a handful of solid puffs, not a soft particle cloud, and they stand
    still while the game is paused.
  - A 3D animal replaces everything the game draws on the animal's sprite: wounds, pack
    saddlebags and a hauled item in its mouth do not show. Its walk is a plain swing of
    straight legs, with no knees, and it does not turn its head or play attack animations.
    Every animal of a kind looks the same (no separate male and female), and a rotting
    carcass looks as fresh as a new one.
  - The grass picture repeats every so many cells, as all ground textures do, so the same
    patches of earth come round again across a big meadow.
  - A weapon in hand is drawn level at hand height. It does not follow the swing or recoil
    animation of the sprite it replaces.
  - 3D trees and plants do not sway in the wind, and bushes do not go bare in winter.
  - A tree's shadow is a simplified shape, not an exact cast of the model, and like every
    shadow in the game it lies on the ground only: it does not fall across walls, and grass
    tufts standing inside it stay lit.
  - Without the shader bundle, the colour looks are blended sheets, not true lookup tables:
    taking colour out also softens contrast a little, "Vivid" cannot add colour, only
    contrast, and the darkest tones lift slightly at night. The LUT versions do not have
    these limits.
  - The water is drawn over the game's own water, so the game's moving water shader and
    its reflections no longer show while the 3D view is on (untick "Stylised water" to get
    them back). The current streaks and sparkles stand still while the game is paused.
  - Roofs are all or nothing; there is no peeking into one room with the roofs on.
  - A turret's gun model turns to aim but does not recoil. Wind turbine blades turn;
    the watermill's wheel does not yet.
  - Wrecks and junk from DLC and mods are matched by name. One with an unusual name
    falls back to being raised from its sprite; say which, and it can be added.
  - A model built for one footprint is stretched to fit a thing of another size, which
    can look squashed for things whose size is very different from the base game's.
  - Grass is one tuft per plant, so a meadow is less dense than the game's own grass. On a
    large, heavily planted map the 3D grass also uses a good deal of memory and makes the
    map take longer to redraw when the view is switched; untick it in the options if so.
  - The floor tiles are drawn for one copy of a texture covering 16 cells each way. If
    tiles do not line up with the map's cells in the game, that number is wrong: say so.
  - With "Cut down walls in front of every room" ticked, which walls are lowered is decided by
    roofs, so a room with no roof keeps all its walls.
  - Floors from DLC and other mods, and burned floors, keep the game's textures.
  - How many cells one copy of a ground texture covers is decided by the game. If the ground
    detail looks too coarse or too fine, say so and the textures can be redrawn to suit.
  - Doors more than one cell wide keep the game's flat drawing.
  - Anything still drawn as an upright sprite (modded races, animals without a model) has
    only the game's four facings and does not know the camera has turned, so seen from
    behind it can appear to walk backwards.
  - Things raised from their own sprite keep that sprite as a lid, painted for a camera
    looking north; from the other three sides its painted front is on the wrong side.
  - Orders dragged out with the mouse (mining, zones, building rows) follow the map's grid,
    so in the isometric view they are diamonds on screen, as in other isometric games.
  - Things without a hand-made model keep their top-down sprite as a lid, which in the
    isometric view lies diagonally like everything else.
  - A wind turbine's blades are a single rotor model turned by the mod; on a broken or
    unpowered turbine they simply stop.
  - Things from DLC and mods without a hand-made model use their raised sprite. Many game
    sprites are painted as if seen slightly from the front, so a tall thing can look like a
    low slab until it gets a model.
  - Ideology wall styles all use the standard wall textures.
  - At night, selection brackets and some glow effects may look dimmer than usual.
  - Other mods that change pawn drawing or the camera have not been tested with it.


6. IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG
---------------------------
  Switch the features off one at a time in the mod options to find which one causes it.
  Turn on the game's development mode to have IsoRim write what it found to the log, then
  look for the lines containing "IsoRim" or "Exception" in:
    %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\Ludeon Studios\RimWorld by Ludeon Studios\Player.log
  On startup the log should contain "[IsoRim] Code loaded.", "[IsoRim] N things have a
  hand-made 3D model." (well over a hundred) and "[IsoRim] Drawing redirected in N methods." Any "[IsoRim] ... was
  not found" line names a feature that switched itself off because the game's code differs
  from what was expected.


7. EXACT COLOUR GRADING (OPTIONAL)
----------------------------------
  The colour looks work without this. For exact grading with lookup tables:
  a. Find the Unity version your game uses: the first lines of Player.log say
     "Initialize engine version: 2022.3.xx". Install that version of Unity (free).
  b. Open the ShaderSource folder of this mod as a Unity project.
  c. Choose IsoRim > Build shader bundles from the menu.
  d. Copy the files it writes (isorimshaders, isorimshaders_mac, isorimshaders_linux) from
     ShaderSource/ShaderBundles into this mod's Shaders folder, and restart the game.
  The log then says "[IsoRim] Exact colour grading (LUT) is available." and the options
  get a "Use lookup tables" tick box and a table picker. Every look has a LUT file in the
  LUTs folder (written by tools/make_luts.py), plus Neutral, Pastel and Cinematic. Any
  1024 x 32 LUT strip (32 slices of 32 x 32; blue picks the slice, red runs across, green
  runs up) put in LUTs can be picked by its file name.


8. CHANGING HOW ONE THING LOOKS
-------------------------------
  A thing only needs an entry in Defs/Models.xml to change how it is drawn. No rebuild is
  needed, only a game restart.

    <IsoRim.ModelDef>
      <defName>R3D_MyThing</defName>
      <thingDef>ThingDefNameHere</thingDef>
      <model>Models/my_thing.obj</model>      use a hand-made model
    </IsoRim.ModelDef>

  Instead of <model>, use <height>0.3</height> to set the height of the automatic shape,
  or <flat>true</flat> to leave the thing as a flat sprite. <builtFor>(3, 2)</builtFor>
  says which footprint the model was made for, so it is stretched to fit the thing.
  <match><li>Wreck</li></match> in place of <thingDef> covers every building or item whose
  name contains one of the words. A turret can have <topModel> (its turning gun, muzzle
  toward +X in Blender) and <topHeight> (the height of its pivot).
  Use <thingCategory> in place of <thingDef> to cover every thing in a category, and
  <models> with several <li> lines to give a thing a choice of shapes. The comment at the
  top of Defs/Models.xml lists every option.

  Making a model (no Unity needed):
  a. Model in Blender. One Blender unit is one map cell. Centre the model on the origin,
     sitting on the ground, with the thing's north side toward +Y in Blender.
     Keep it low: a few hundred triangles at most.
  b. Give each part a material. Its Base Color is used, and if the Base Color has an image
     texture from this mod's Textures folder, that texture is used too (UV-unwrap the part;
     textures repeat, one repeat per Blender unit matches the supplied models).
     Name a material starting with "stuff" (stuff, stuff_dark ...) to have it take the
     colour of what the thing is built from.
  c. File > Export > Wavefront (.obj), default settings, into the Models folder.

  The supplied models and textures are generated by tools/make_models.py (with
  tools/models_more.py, which holds the furniture, machines, items and wrecks added in
  version 13) and tools/make_textures.py (Python 3; the textures need numpy and Pillow).
  Edit the shapes or the drawing code there and run them to rewrite Models/, Textures/
  and Defs/Models.xml. tools/make_luts.py writes the LUT files, and
  tools/preview_models.py OUTDIR draws contact sheets of the models for checking them
  without starting the game.
  The wall textures are plain PNG files in Textures/IsoRim/Walls and can also be repainted
  by hand: keep them light grey, and keep the left/right and top/bottom edges matching.

  Defs/        key bindings and the per-thing settings

  Textures/    the mod's own wall, ground, model and button textures

PERFORMANCE NOTES
-----------------
- People and animals drawn as 3D models skip the game's own pawn rendering entirely
  (RenderPawnAt prefix returns false, ParallelPreRenderPawnAt is skipped), so the
  sprite pipeline costs nothing for them. Portraits still use the game's renderer.
- Each person's look is merged into two cached meshes (body, head) keyed by facing,
  posture and light step, so a figure is two draw calls instead of ~16.
- Walls and trees live in their own section layers (SectionLayer_IsoRimWalls/Trees),
  so see-through cutaways only rebuild those, never the full things layer.
- Section rebuilds are time-budgeted (MapSections.Process, 4 ms per frame).
- Water sparkles use a cached water grid and only walk the visible cells.
- About/ModIcon.png is made by tools/make_icon.py.

ITEMS, WEAPONS AND ARMOUR
-------------------------
- With "Items and weapons use the game's art" on (default), every item except chunks is made
  solid from its own sprite (Forms.Compute -> Form3D.Extruded, thickness Forms.ItemThickness).
  The hand-made item models are only used with that setting off.
- Held weapons are thin slabs built from the weapon sprite (SpriteSolids.cs): the sprite on both
  faces, sides coloured from its outline (Extruder.MaskOf), stood on edge and pointed at the aim.
- Worn armour: person_<build>_armor_<kind> (flakvest, flakjacket, flakpants, plate, marine,
  cataphract, recon) and person_hat_<kind> (combat, marine_helm, cataphract_helm, recon_helm),
  chosen by defName in People.ArmourKind/HatKind. Clothing not made from stuff takes the average
  colour of its own sprite (People.ApparelColour).

FLICKER (Z-FIGHTING) RULES
--------------------------
- Shape.separate() in make_models.py lifts any face that shares a plane with a face of another
  material by 0.003, so no two overlapping faces sit at the same depth.
- Textured faces stand ModelBaker.FaceLift off their plain solid copy (different shaders do not
  always produce identical depth).
- The outline material has _ZBias 1. Dynamic meshes are retired and destroyed two frames later.

LIGHTING (SMOOTH SUN)
---------------------
- The sun moves continuously with the hour (R3DMaterials.SetSunHour, called every frame).
- Static geometry is never re-baked for light. Every face goes into one of 26 direction groups
  (R3DMaterials.BinOf); each group has its own material (Lit for plain vertex colours,
  LitTexture for textures) whose _Color is set from the sun. curve 1 = wall/rock shading.
- Moving things (people, animals, doors) still bake shading into cached meshes, rebuilt once per
  game hour (R3DMaterials.LightStep / Shade, which use the sun as it was at the step).
- Roof textures use the same groups; their brightness is applied in the roof DrawLayer.

GHOSTS, WORK, DAMAGE
--------------------
- Ghosts.cs: blueprints (blue) and frames (sawdust) are baked as see-through ghosts
  (ModelBaker.Ghost); the placement preview is drawn every frame (GhostDrawer.DrawGhostThing postfix).
- Work.cs (class WorkAnimations): job -> tool and motion (chop/sweep/bob); tools are Models/tool_*.obj.
- SectionLayer_IsoRimDamage (Layers.cs): marks from Textures/IsoRim/Damage/Marks.png (tools/make_damage.py)
  on model faces (ModelBaker.DamageMarks), wall caps/sides and extruded tops. The game's flat
  damage layer is hidden while it is on.
- Walls/trees layers are queued for redraw from Thing.SpawnSetup/DeSpawn (deconstruct, mine, cut).

POINTER AND FOG (Interaction.cs)
--------------------------------
- Drag box drawn on the ground (DragBoxOnGUI skipped in 3D); it selects the same world rectangle.
- Selection brackets drawn on the ground round the footprint (SelectionDrawer.DrawSelectionBracketFor).
- GenUI.ThingsUnderMouse postfix: a ray from the pointer is marched down from 3.2 cells; things whose
  3D height the ray passes through are put first, nearest the camera first.
- Ghosts for every cell of a build drag (DesignationDragger.DragCells).
- Fog: each fog sheet's corners are moved onto the top of the rock/walls/buildings under them, redone
  every 40 frames; put back flat when the 3D view is off. (View3D.FogMatrix is no longer used.)
- Setting itemThickness scales item extrusion; 0 lays items flat.

FLOATING ICONS
--------------
- DrawHook.billboard: while a scope is open, each mesh drawn is stood upright at its own centre,
  facing the camera, and raised by billboardHeight. Used for OverlayDrawer status icons (raised to
  the thing's pick height + 0.3), speech/thought bubbles and overhead motes (1.45 cells), and
  designation icons (Verse.Designation is added to the redirected types). DrawBatch.DrawMesh gets
  the same transform, for icons the game draws in batches.

RIMWORLD 1.5 AND 1.6
--------------------
- One source tree, built twice: build.bat runs `dotnet build -p:RimWorldVersion=1.6` and `=1.5`,
  writing 1.6/Assemblies and 1.5/Assemblies. About/LoadFolders.xml loads the shared top level plus
  the matching version folder. The symbol RW16 is defined for 1.6 builds, for any code that has
  to differ (#if RW16). There must be no Assemblies folder at the top level.
- Uncertain game APIs are looked up by name with reflection and skipped with a dev-mode warning
  when missing, so a renamed method switches that feature off instead of breaking the mod.
